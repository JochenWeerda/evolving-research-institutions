# Evolving Research Institutions (ERI)

**Scaling verified discovery with millions of ephemeral AI agents**

ERI is an open research proposal for building large populations of AI agents that do more than generate vast amounts of redundant output. Its central claim is that cumulative discovery requires durable institutions, independent verification, and an economic rule that converts reproduced knowledge—not activity—into future compute.

> Ephemeral agents work inside durable institutions, and only independently reproduced knowledge funds the next compute generation.

## Why this proposal exists

A million parallel agents can produce more attempts than a hundred agents. It can also produce more duplication, correlated errors, reward hacking, and unverifiable claims. Raw population size is therefore a necessary scaling variable, but not a sufficient mechanism for progress.

ERI treats **institutional organization** as the missing scaling layer:

- agents are temporary workers with bounded compute and permissions;
- institutions persist across agent lifetimes and inherit memory, methods, governance, reputation, and compute treasuries;
- claims carry evidence, uncertainty, artifacts, and provenance;
- independent institutions reproduce, falsify, and assess novelty;
- verified claims earn compute rights for both discoverers and verifiers;
- successful institutional designs may split, merge, recruit, vary, or be archived;
- consolidation updates memories, skills, and tested adapters before any consideration of shared base-weight changes.

## The scale thesis

The long-range target is not dozens of agents. It is a federated ecology capable of supporting **millions of concurrent or episodic agent instances**. Small experiments are only the validation ladder: the incentive mechanism must demonstrate positive verified-knowledge scaling before the population is enlarged.

Naive scaling optimizes the number of attempts. ERI instead optimizes:

\[
\eta = \frac{\text{independently verified, novel, useful knowledge}}{\text{compute} + \text{verification cost} + \text{safety cost}}
\]

The architecture should scale only while verified knowledge grows faster than redundancy, verification burden, concentration, and risk.

## What may be new

Large agent populations, agent economies, knowledge markets, shared memory, replay, group selection, and evolving rules each have substantial prior art. ERI does not claim those ingredients individually.

The narrower research contribution is their closed institutional selection loop:

1. durable research institutions are the heritable units;
2. their compute treasuries grow through independently reproduced claims;
3. institutional memory, methods, and governance evolve together;
4. selection occurs at both agent and institution levels;
5. anti-duplication and verification capacity are first-class scaling constraints.

This is a hypothesis to test, not a claim of proven novelty or superiority.

## Proposed first experiment

Start with 64–128 agents and eight institutions across six controlled comparison arms. Use hidden-ground-truth tasks, equal model and compute budgets, blind replication, and complete claim provenance. Scale through progressively larger stages only if ERI improves verified knowledge per unit of compute without unacceptable concentration or safety failures.

The small pilot tests the mechanism. It does not redefine the intended scale.

## Status

Version 0.1 is a conceptual, falsifiable research proposal. It includes no empirical performance claim and no autonomous deployment recommendation.
